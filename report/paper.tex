\title{\vspace{-1.4cm}\textbf{Unlearning a group from an in-context prompt:\\
how much does corrupting it actually remove?}}
\author{}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle
\vspace{-1.3cm}

\begin{abstract}
\noindent
We ask whether a group of training examples can be unlearned from a
linear-attention in-context learner by \emph{corrupting that group inside the
prompt}, with no weight update, and whether a membership adversary is actually
defeated or merely blinded. Sweeping six corruption families over four decades
of strength, on a synthetic Gaussian task and on real MNIST features, across
nine seed combinations, we find that \textbf{the membership signal lives in the
first moment of the residual}. Additive-noise corruptions cannot reach it: they
drive the adversary toward chance only by inflating variance, and a
shared-noise control attributes $84$--$100\%$ of that movement to masking
rather than removal. Reaching chance this way costs $762$--$1699\times$ in
preservation error on the synthetic task and $4375$--$7838\times$ on MNIST.
Corruptions that move the \emph{mean} --- label flips --- reach chance $6\times$
(synthetic) to $16$--$21\times$ (MNIST) more cheaply. We derive the flip's
effect on the read-out in closed form, exactly, which makes the membership AUC
a differentiable function of the flip probability and yields
$\theta^*=(N+1)\delta_0/2\sum_i a_i$ for the optimal flip. Optimising it lands
on chance at $\mathrm{AUC}=0.5013\pm0.0005$ on MNIST, reproducibly across every
seed, at $\varepsilon\approx0.05$; the closed form agrees with the brief's
REINFORCE estimator and is $7$--$9\times$ faster. Where the required flip
fraction exceeds one --- as it does on one synthetic architecture --- no label
flip can cancel the gap even in principle, and we show the optimiser saturating
against that boundary rather than converging.
\end{abstract}

\section{Problem}

\begin{mdframed}[linewidth=0.8pt,linecolor=black!55,backgroundcolor=black!3,
  innertopmargin=7pt,innerbottommargin=7pt,innerleftmargin=9pt,innerrightmargin=9pt]
\textbf{Thesis.} In this model the membership signal lives in the \emph{first
moment} of the residual. A corruption therefore erases it only if it moves the
mean; every variance-based corruption can at best \emph{mask} a signal it
cannot reach, and pays for the masking in preservation error. The optimal
corruption is the one that cancels the baseline mean gap --- which we derive in
closed form, and which correctly predicts the learned flip probability wherever
the required cancellation is achievable.
\end{mdframed}

A model is shown a prompt containing examples from three groups and must
predict the query. One group is to be \emph{forgotten}. We never touch the
weights --- we corrupt that group's tokens in the prompt and ask whether a
membership adversary can still tell that the group was trained on.

The eight results below are evidence for the boxed claim rather than a list of
things that did or did not work. The negative results are not failures of
particular corruptions; they are a map of where the signal is not.

Two quantities are in tension throughout.

\textbf{Removal.} An adversary sees the sign-aligned residual
$r=\mathrm{sign}(y)(\hat y - y)$ at a fixed probe point and must decide which
of two models produced it: $\Mfull$, trained on all three groups, or $\Morac$,
retrained without the forget group. We report the Mann--Whitney statistic
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{AUC}
= \Pr\big[\,r(\Mfull) > r(\Morac)\,\big] + \tfrac12\Pr\big[\,r(\Mfull)=r(\Morac)\,\big],
\end{equation}
so $\mathrm{AUC}=\tfrac12$ means the adversary is guessing. \emph{Both models
are scored on the same corrupted prompt with the same noise draw}, so
detecting the edit cannot be mistaken for detecting membership.

The ordering is fixed a priori: we always ask whether $\Mfull$'s residual
exceeds $\Morac$'s, never the reverse. Because the full model fits
forget-group queries better its residual is \emph{smaller}, so the baseline
sits \emph{below} $\tfrac12$ --- equivalently, the $0.375$ reported later is
$0.625$ under the usual membership-inference orientation. Signal strength is
$|\mathrm{AUC}-\tfrac12|$ and the unlearning target is $\tfrac12$ under either
convention. We do not symmetrise: $\max(a,1-a)$ is biased upward under the
null (it reads $\approx0.54$ when the truth is $0.50$), which would
manufacture signal at precisely the point these experiments measure.

\textbf{Preservation.} How far the corrupted model has drifted from the
retrain oracle it is meant to imitate:
\begin{equation}
\varepsilon = \mathrm{KL}\big(p_{\mathrm{oracle}} \,\|\, p_{\mathrm{unlearned}}\big)
\end{equation}
on the forget-population residual law, which is fit as a univariate Gaussian
$(\mu,v)$ over the probe population, so $\varepsilon$ is a Gaussian KL.
Unlearning is only useful if $\mathrm{AUC}\to\tfrac12$ \emph{and}
$\varepsilon$ stays small.

\paragraph{Are AUC and $\varepsilon$ the same measurement?} Both are functions
of the first two moments, so it is fair to ask whether the cost ratios below
are tautological. They are not, and the reason is the mechanism this report is
about. AUC depends only on the \emph{standardised} gap
$(\mu_1-\mu_0)/\sqrt{v_1+v_0}$ and is invariant to rescaling both populations
together; $\varepsilon$ is not scale-invariant and grows with the variance
ratio. A corruption that inflates $v$ therefore drives AUC to $\tfrac12$ while
$\varepsilon$ keeps climbing --- which is exactly the masking regime, and
exactly why the two can be traded against each other at all.

\paragraph{Corruptions.} Applied to the forget tokens $(x_f,y_f)$ only:
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{llll}
\toprule
name & edit & parameter & channel\\
\midrule
C1 & $y_f \to y_f + \epsilon$, \ $\epsilon\sim\mathcal N(0,\vs)$ & $\vs$ & variance\\
C2 & $x_f \to x_f + \epsilon$, \ $\epsilon\sim\mathcal N(0,\vs I)$ & $\vs$ & variance\\
C3 & both, independent draws & $\vs$ & variance\\
flip & $y_f \to (1-2t)\,y_f$ & $t$ & mean\\
bern & $y_f \to (1-2B)\,y_f$, \ $B\sim\mathrm{Bern}(\theta)$ & $\theta$ & mean + variance\\
whiten & $x_f \to$ covariance-matched to retain groups & mixing & --- \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The last two matter most. \texttt{flip} carries a mean shift with \emph{no}
variance; \texttt{bern} has the same mean, $\mathbb E[(1-2B)y]=(1-2\theta)y$,
plus variance $4\theta(1-\theta)y^2$. Writing the predicted AUC as
$\Phi\!\big((\mu_1-\mu_0)/\sqrt{v_1+v_0}\big)$ makes the difference concrete:
a zero-mean corruption can only inflate the denominator, so it can never push
AUC through $\tfrac12$, while a mean shift moves the numerator directly.

\section{Method}

\needspacehack
\begin{algbox}
\algtitle{Algorithm 1 — measuring removal and preservation}
\small
\textbf{Input:} group spec, forget group $f$, corruption mode and grid\\[3pt]
\textbf{1.} Train two shadow ensembles of $S=512$ linear-attention models:
$\Mfull$ on all groups, $\Morac$ on the retain groups only.\\
\textbf{2.} Draw a probe of $P=64$ prompts and \emph{freeze} it.\\
\textbf{3.} For each corruption strength $t$ in the grid:\\
\hspace*{1.4em}(a) corrupt the forget tokens of the probe $\to$ $\tilde X$;\\
\hspace*{1.4em}(b) run \emph{both} ensembles on the same $\tilde X$;\\
\hspace*{1.4em}(c) $\mathrm{AUC}(t)$ from the two residual populations;\\
\hspace*{1.4em}(d) $\varepsilon(t)$ from the fitted residual laws;\\
\hspace*{1.4em}(e) \emph{shared-noise control}: repeat (a)--(c) broadcasting one
noise draw across all $S$ shadows. This holds variance inflation fixed, so the
gap between the two runs separates \emph{masking} from genuine removal.\\
\textbf{4.} Repeat for $3\times3$ (training seed, probe seed) combinations;
report medians.
\end{algbox}

Nothing retrains inside the loop: the corruption is a prompt-time edit, so a
whole sweep costs seconds against cached ensembles.

\paragraph{A closed form for the flip.} The brief derives a REINFORCE
estimator for Stage 2 because AUC is not differentiable through the sampling.
For this policy that is avoidable. With $a_i = y_i\,(c_x\!\cdot\! x_i)$ over
the forget slice, the induced change in the read-out has moments
\begin{equation}
\mathbb E[\Delta\hat y] = \frac{-2\theta}{N+1}\sum_i a_i,
\qquad
\mathrm{Var}[\Delta\hat y] = \frac{4\theta(1-\theta)}{(N+1)^2}\sum_i a_i^2 ,
\label{eq:moments}
\end{equation}
\emph{exactly} --- because $(1-2B)^2=1$ leaves the label--label block of the
context vector untouched. Feeding \eqref{eq:moments} into the Gaussian AUC
makes $\mathrm{AUC}(\theta)$ an ordinary differentiable function of $\theta$.
Monte Carlo at $4\times10^5$ trials per point confirms both moments to 3--4
significant figures.

\begin{algbox}
\algtitle{Algorithm 2 — learning the corruption (Stage 2)}
\small
The brief writes $\min_\theta \mathrm{AUC}(\theta)+\lambda\|\theta\|$. Every
measured AUC here is \emph{below} $\tfrac12$, so minimising AUC drives toward
\emph{more} distinguishability ($0.30$ is a better adversary than $0.50$,
merely inverted). We minimise distance from chance instead:
\begin{equation*}
\min_\theta\ \big(\mathrm{AUC}(\theta)-\tfrac12\big)^2 + \lambda\|\theta\| .
\end{equation*}
\textbf{1.} Initialise $\theta$; \ \textbf{2.} $\mathrm{AUC}(\theta)$ from
\eqref{eq:moments} in closed form; \ \textbf{3.} backprop and Adam step;
\ \textbf{4.} repeat 400 steps; \ \textbf{5.} report AUC and $\varepsilon$
\emph{measured} by Algorithm 1, not the closed-form value.
\end{algbox}

\small
\textbf{Reading the numbers.}
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}\setlength{\topsep}{2pt}
\item $\mathrm{AUC}=\tfrac12$ is the \emph{goal} --- the adversary guessing. The
baseline sits \emph{below} $\tfrac12$ because the full model fits the forget
group better and so ranks under the oracle; $0.375$ is a strong signal, not a
weak one. Unlearning means driving AUC \emph{up} to $\tfrac12$.
\item $\varepsilon$ small is good. It is a KL against the retrain oracle, so
$\varepsilon\!\to\!0$ means ``indistinguishable from having retrained properly''.
\item \textbf{Cost ratio} $=\varepsilon$ at the closest approach to chance,
divided by $\varepsilon$ at its own minimum. It is the price of defeating the
adversary, and it is the number the whole report turns on.
\item Every value is a median over nine (training seed, probe seed)
combinations; bands are min--max across those nine.
\end{itemize}

\label{fig:e1frontier}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Result 1 (negative).} No arm offers a clean operating point.
Reaching chance costs between two and three orders of magnitude in
preservation error. Figure~\ref{fig:e1frontier} shows why in one picture: the
frontier is flat while removal is cheap, then turns almost vertical.

\paragraph{Result 2.} Most of the apparent progress is \emph{masking}, not
removal. The shared-noise control attributes $100\%$, $84\%$ and $87\%$ of the
movement at $\vs=0.32,0.56,1$ to variance inflation hiding the signal, falling
to $41\%$ only at $\vs=100$. (Below $\vs\approx0.1$ the decomposition is
undefined --- AUC does not move toward chance there at all --- and no claim
here rests on that region.)

\paragraph{Result 3.} The arms are not equally bad, and they separate by
\emph{which moment} they move. The Gaussian arms never exceed $0.499$; the
deterministic flip rises \emph{through} chance to $0.527$ and $0.539$, exactly
as the $\Phi$ argument above predicts. Whitening barely moves at all
($0.465\to0.471$). The stochastic flip, carrying both channels, is the
cheapest arm in the table by a factor of $\sim\!7$.

\section{Experiment 2 — replication on real data}

\subsection*{How MNIST enters the experiment}

MNIST supplies the \emph{input distributions}, not the task. This is not digit
classification, and the construction is worth stating precisely because it is
easy to assume otherwise.

\paragraph{Three groups.} Take digits $1$, $3$ and $8$; PCA the \emph{pooled}
set down to $D=4$ so all three share one feature space; split back by digit;
centre each class and normalise it so $\operatorname{tr}(\mathrm{Cov})=1$. The
result is three \emph{banks} of 4-dimensional vectors --- roughly $6.7$k, $6.1$k
and $5.9$k rows, one per image. A group is therefore a cloud of thousands of
points, and because the traces are equal the clouds differ only in covariance
\emph{shape}, summarised by the participation ratio
$\mathrm{PR}=(\operatorname{tr}\Lambda)^2/\operatorname{tr}(\Lambda^2)$:
$2.44$, $3.29$, $2.93$. \textbf{Retain $=\{d_1,d_3\}$, forget $=d_8$.} Note the
forget group is the middle one, not an extreme --- unlike the synthetic setup,
the geometry here is whatever the data happened to provide.

\paragraph{The labels come from a random plane, not from the digit.} For each
prompt we draw a fresh $\beta\sim\mathcal N(0,I_4)$ and set
$y=\mathrm{sign}(\beta^{\!\top}x)$. The label alphabet is fixed at $\pm1$, but
the labelling \emph{function} changes every prompt, so the same image receives
different labels in different prompts. A fixed image$\to$digit rule would be
memorisable in the weights, leaving nothing to learn in context; a fresh
$\beta$ forces the model to recover the plane from the examples in front of it.

\paragraph{$\beta$ is shared across the three groups.} This is the load-bearing
constraint. If each digit carried its own labelling rule, corrupting the forget
group would delete the answer rather than the membership evidence, and a fall
in AUC would measure nothing. With $\beta$ shared, all three groups obey one
rule and differ \emph{only} in input geometry --- which is precisely the
quantity the attack is being asked to detect. It also keeps the retrain oracle
competent on forget-group queries: it can label a $d_8$ point perfectly well,
having simply never seen that class's geometry during training. Had the digit
been the label, the oracle could not answer at all and membership inference
would collapse into trivial capability detection.

\paragraph{Probe layout.} Each of the $P=64$ prompts is
\begin{center}\small
$\underbrace{[\,10\ \text{from } d_1\,][\,10\ \text{from } d_3\,][\,11\ \text{from } d_8\,]}_{N=31\ \text{context tokens}}
\ \longrightarrow\ \underbrace{1\ \text{more } d_8\ \text{point}}_{\text{query}}$
\end{center}
Forget tokens are placed last so they form a contiguous slice, and the
\emph{query is drawn from the forget group} --- the model is asked about the
distribution we are trying to make it forget, which is where the membership
signal appears. Every corruption arm edits only those 11 $d_8$ context tokens:
never $d_1$, never $d_3$, never the query. Everything else --- the two $512$-model
ensembles, the attack, the sweep, the shared-noise control --- is unchanged from
Experiment 1.

question here is whether the conclusion survives when the input clouds are real

happen to be --- rather than designed.

\paragraph{Result 6 (positive).} On MNIST the learned flip lands at
$\mathrm{AUC}=0.5013\pm0.0005$ --- chance, to within a twentieth of a

against $\varepsilon\approx1.0$ for Gaussian noise to reach the same place.
Three independent routes agree: the closed form gives $\theta=0.678/0.753$, a
41-point grid search gives $0.675/0.725$, and REINFORCE lands within $0.03$.

\paragraph{Result 7 (limitation).} It does not transfer to the synthetic task.
There the two estimators diverge wildly --- $\theta=0.850$ versus $0.016$ on
ATTN-M --- and neither reaches chance. The closed form's Bernoulli error is
$3.2\times10^{-3}$ there against $1.1\times10^{-3}$ on MNIST. Result 9 below
gives the reason, and it is structural rather than numerical.

\paragraph{Result 8 --- and a closed form for $\theta^*$.} The optimum flips
$68$--$82\%$ of forget labels: not a small perturbation, but the
\emph{mean-compensating} solution. Setting the corrupted mean gap to zero,
$\delta_0 + \mathbb E[\Delta\hat y] = 0$ with $\mathbb E[\Delta\hat y]$ from
\eqref{eq:moments}, gives
\begin{equation}
\theta^* \;=\; \frac{(N+1)\,\delta_0}{2\sum_i a_i},
\label{eq:thetastar}
\end{equation}
where $\delta_0$ is the baseline mean gap. Note what \eqref{eq:thetastar} does
\emph{not} contain: the variance. The extra spread $4\theta(1-\theta)\sum a_i^2$
sits in the denominator of the AUC and cannot move the point where the
numerator vanishes.

That yields a prediction testable on data already in hand. The deterministic
\texttt{flip} at strength $t$ has the \emph{same} mean shift and no variance at
all, so its AUC must cross $\tfrac12$ at exactly $t=\theta^*$. Reading that
crossing off the \texttt{flip} sweep predicts the learned $\theta^*$ with no
fitting:

\paragraph{Result 9 --- why the synthetic task fails, structurally.} The last
row of Table~\ref{tab:thetastar} is the explanation for Result 7. Cancelling
the baseline gap on synthetic ATTN-S requires $t=1.011$. But $\theta$ is a
\emph{probability}: $\theta\le1$. \textbf{The required mean cancellation lies
outside the range a label flip can deliver}, so \texttt{bern} cannot reach
chance there in principle --- and indeed it never does, topping out at $0.4979$.
The optimiser returning $\theta^*=0.895\pm0.091$ is not converging badly; it is
saturating against a boundary. Synthetic ATTN-M sits just inside the feasible
region and is correspondingly noisy.

This also explains the estimator disagreement quantitatively. Pushing
$\theta^*$ toward 1 drives the flip's mean shift --- a sum of $n_f=11$ Bernoulli
terms --- into its degenerate corner, where the variance vanishes and the
standardised third moment diverges. At the fitted optima the skew of that sum
is $-0.37$ and $-0.49$ on MNIST against $-0.33$ and $-0.78$ on synthetic, and
on the worst synthetic seed ($\theta=0.964$) it reaches $-1.50$ with excess
kurtosis $+2.07$. The Gaussian AUC underlying \eqref{eq:moments} is therefore
weakest exactly where the synthetic optimum is forced to sit. (These are
equal-weight values; unequal $a_i$ inflate them further, and the synthetic
task's continuous labels give a much wider spread of $a_i$ than MNIST's
$\pm1$.)

\paragraph{In one sentence.} Context-level unlearning cannot remove group
membership, only mask it --- unless the corruption moves the exact moment the
signal lives in, which for this model is the mean; the optimal such corruption
is the one cancelling the baseline gap, it is available in closed form, and it
works wherever that cancellation is achievable at all.

\paragraph{Scope.} This is \emph{group-level} unlearning: the adversary
detects whether a distribution was trained on, not whether a specific example
was. Two datasets, one architecture family, scalar policy only --- the
conditional policy $p_\theta(\cdot\mid x_f,y_f)$ is implemented but not
reported here. Fixing Result 7 by polishing the closed-form optimum against
measured AUC is the obvious next step.

\paragraph{Two bugs found and fixed.} Stage 2's measurement originally treated
the flip probability $\theta$ as if it were a realised draw, computing
$(1-2\theta)y_f$ --- which is the deterministic \texttt{flip} arm, not the
stochastic one. It was caught by cross-checking against Experiment 1: the
Stage 2 grid search reproduced the \texttt{flip} curve exactly ($0.4751$)
instead of the \texttt{bern} curve ($0.4821$). Separately, the sweep script
hardcoded an eigenbasis field that MNIST specs do not have, so the MNIST
sweep crashed on bookkeeping after computing every result. All numbers above
are post-fix.

\section{Derivation of the flip moments and of $\theta^*$}
\label{app:deriv}

Equation~\eqref{eq:moments} is stated in Section 2 without proof. It is four
lines.

\paragraph{Step 1 --- the read-out is linear in the context labels.} For linear
attention performing in-context regression, the prediction at the query is a
weighted sum of the context labels,
\[
\hat y_q \;=\; \frac{1}{N+1}\sum_i y_i\,(c_x\!\cdot\! x_i),
\qquad c_x = M^{\!\top} x_q .
\]
Write $a_i = y_i\,(c_x\!\cdot\!x_i)$ for the signed contribution of context
token $i$, so that $\hat y_q = \frac{1}{N+1}\sum_i a_i$. Linearity in $y$ is
the only property used below.

\paragraph{Step 2 --- what the flip does.} The policy replaces $y_i$ by
$(1-2B_i)y_i$ for $i$ in the forget slice, with $B_i\sim\mathrm{Bern}(\theta)$
drawn independently. Token $i$'s contribution becomes $(1-2B_i)a_i$, so the
change in the prediction is
\[
\Delta\hat y \;=\; \frac{1}{N+1}\sum_{i\in f}\big[(1-2B_i)-1\big]a_i
\;=\; \frac{-2}{N+1}\sum_{i\in f} B_i\,a_i .
\]
A weighted sum of independent Bernoulli variables.

\paragraph{Step 3 --- its moments.} With $\mathbb E[B_i]=\theta$ and
$\mathrm{Var}(B_i)=\theta(1-\theta)$, independence gives
\eqref{eq:moments} immediately:
\[
\mathbb E[\Delta\hat y] = \frac{-2\theta}{N+1}\sum_i a_i,
\qquad
\mathrm{Var}[\Delta\hat y] = \frac{4\theta(1-\theta)}{(N+1)^2}\sum_i a_i^2 .
\]

\paragraph{Step 4 --- why this is exact, not first-order.} The context vector
also carries a label--label block, terms in $\sum_i y_i^2$. Under the flip
those become $\sum_i (1-2B_i)^2 y_i^2 = \sum_i y_i^2$, because
$(1-2B)^2 = 1$ for $B\in\{0,1\}$: the block is \emph{unchanged}. No
second-order correction survives, so the moments hold exactly for every
$\theta$. Contrast additive noise, $y\to y+\epsilon$, which sends
$\sum y^2 \to \sum y^2 + 2\sum y\epsilon + \sum\epsilon^2$ and leaves terms
behind.

\paragraph{Consequence 1 --- a differentiable AUC.} Modelling the two residual
populations as Gaussian, $\mathrm{AUC} = \Phi\big((\mu_1-\mu_0)/\sqrt{v_1+v_0}\big)$,
and \eqref{eq:moments} supplies $\mu_1(\theta)$ and $v_1(\theta)$ in closed
form. $\mathrm{AUC}(\theta)$ is therefore an explicit smooth function and can
be differentiated directly --- no sampling, no gradient variance. This is
Algorithm 2.

\paragraph{Consequence 2 --- the optimum.} Setting the corrupted mean gap to
zero, $\delta_0 + \mathbb E[\Delta\hat y] = 0$, and solving gives
\eqref{eq:thetastar}, $\theta^* = (N+1)\delta_0 / (2\sum_i a_i)$. The variance
term does not appear: it sits in the denominator of the AUC and cannot move
the point at which the numerator vanishes. That is why the deterministic
\texttt{flip} --- same mean shift, no variance --- must cross $\tfrac12$ at the
same parameter value, which is the prediction tested in
Table~\ref{tab:thetastar}.

\paragraph{Verification.} \texttt{tests/verify\_bern.py} draws $4\times10^5$
Monte Carlo realisations per grid point and recovers both moments to 3--4
significant figures, and confirms $\sum\tilde y^2 - \sum y^2 = 0$ exactly.
