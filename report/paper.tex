\title{\vspace{-1.4cm}\textbf{Unlearning a group from an in-context prompt:\\
how much does corrupting it actually remove?}}
\author{}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle
\vspace{-1.3cm}

\begin{abstract}
\noindent
We test whether a group of training examples can be unlearned from a
linear-attention in-context learner by corrupting that group inside the prompt,
without changing any weights. The question is whether a membership adversary is
actually defeated or only blinded. We sweep six corruption families over four
decades of strength, on a synthetic Gaussian task and on MNIST features, across
nine seed combinations.

The membership signal sits in the first moment of the residual. Additive noise
cannot reach it. It moves the adversary toward chance by inflating variance
instead, and a shared-noise control attributes $84$--$100\%$ of that movement to
masking rather than removal. Reaching chance this way costs $762$--$1699\times$
in preservation error on the synthetic task and $4375$--$7838\times$ on MNIST.

Corruptions that move the mean, such as label flips, reach chance $6\times$
(synthetic) to $16$--$21\times$ (MNIST) more cheaply. We derive the flip's
effect on the read-out exactly. This makes membership AUC a differentiable
function of the flip probability and gives $\theta^*=(N+1)\delta_0/2\sum_i a_i$
for the optimal flip. Optimising it reaches
$\mathrm{AUC}=0.4987\pm0.0005$ on MNIST at $\varepsilon\approx0.05$, with the
same value on every seed. The closed form agrees with the REINFORCE estimator
and runs $7$--$9\times$ faster. On one synthetic architecture the required flip
fraction is above one, so no label flip can cancel the gap at all; there the
optimiser saturates against that bound instead of converging.
\end{abstract}

\section{Problem}

\begin{mdframed}[linewidth=0.8pt,linecolor=black!55,backgroundcolor=black!3,
  innertopmargin=7pt,innerbottommargin=7pt,innerleftmargin=9pt,innerrightmargin=9pt]
\textbf{Claim.} In this model the membership signal sits in the first moment
of the residual. A corruption erases it only if it moves the mean. A
variance-based corruption can mask the signal but cannot reach it, and pays for
the masking in preservation error. The best corruption is the one that cancels
the baseline mean gap. We derive it in closed form, and it predicts the learned
flip probability wherever that cancellation is possible.
\end{mdframed}

A model is shown a prompt containing examples from three groups and must
predict the query. One group is to be forgotten. We do not touch the weights.
We corrupt that group's tokens in the prompt and ask whether a membership
adversary can still tell the group was trained on.

The results below are evidence for the claim above. The negative ones show
where the signal is not, which is what makes the positive one interpretable.

Two quantities trade off against each other.

\textbf{Removal.} An adversary sees the sign-aligned residual
$r=\mathrm{sign}(y)(\hat y - y)$ at a fixed probe point and must decide which
of two models produced it: $\Mfull$, trained on all three groups, or $\Morac$,
retrained without the forget group.

The full model has seen the forget group, so it fits forget-group queries
better and its residual is \emph{smaller}. Membership-inference convention puts
the member on the high side of the score, so the membership score is the
negated residual,
\begin{equation}
s \;=\; -r \;=\; -\,\mathrm{sign}(y)\,(\hat y - y),
\end{equation}
and we report the Mann--Whitney statistic on $s$:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{AUC}
= \Pr\big[\,s(\Mfull) > s(\Morac)\,\big]
+ \tfrac12\Pr\big[\,s(\Mfull)=s(\Morac)\,\big].
\end{equation}
A working attack therefore reads $\mathrm{AUC}>\tfrac12$, chance is
$\tfrac12$, and unlearning means driving AUC \emph{down} to $\tfrac12$. The
ordering is fixed in the code rather than chosen per run, so it cannot be tuned
after the fact.

\emph{Both models are scored on the same corrupted prompt with the same noise
draw}, so detecting the edit cannot be mistaken for detecting membership. We do
not symmetrise: $\max(a,1-a)$ is biased upward under the null (it reads
$\approx0.54$ when the truth is $0.50$), which would manufacture signal at
exactly the point these experiments measure.

\textbf{Preservation.} How far the corrupted model has drifted from the
retrain oracle it is meant to imitate. Fitting the forget-population residual
law as a univariate Gaussian $(\mu,v)$ over the probe gives two Gaussian KLs,
\begin{equation}
\alpha = \mathrm{KL}\big(p_{\mathrm{full}} \,\|\, p_{\mathrm{unlearned}}\big),
\qquad
\varepsilon = \mathrm{KL}\big(p_{\mathrm{oracle}} \,\|\, p_{\mathrm{unlearned}}\big).
\end{equation}
$\alpha$ measures \emph{removal}: how far the edit has moved the model away
from its uncorrupted self, so larger is better. $\varepsilon$ measures
\emph{preservation}: how far it sits from the retrain oracle, so smaller is
better. Unlearning is only useful if $\mathrm{AUC}\to\tfrac12$ \emph{and}
$\varepsilon$ stays small. The two are plotted against each other in the
removal/preservation planes of Figure~\ref{fig:e1frontier} and Appendix~C.

\paragraph{The model.} One layer of linear self-attention doing in-context
regression. Writing $M=W_QW_K^{\!\top}$ and $t_i$ for the $i$th context token,
the prediction at the query is
\begin{equation}
\hat y_q = \frac{1}{N+1}\, t_q^{\!\top} M u ,
\qquad u = \sum_i t_i\,y_i ,
\label{eq:readout}
\end{equation}
so the read-out is \emph{linear in the context labels}. That is the only
property the derivation in Appendix~\ref{app:deriv} uses. We write
$c_x = M^{\!\top}x_q$ for the resulting weight on input $x_i$.

The two architectures share this forward pass exactly and differ only in how
$M$ is parameterised, hence in their training dynamics: \textbf{ATTN-S} keeps
the factored form $M=W_QW_K^{\!\top}$, \textbf{ATTN-M} learns the merged $M$
directly. They are carried through every experiment as two independent
instances of the same phenomenon, not as a comparison of interest in itself.

\paragraph{Are AUC and $\varepsilon$ the same measurement?} Both depend on the
first two moments, so the cost ratios below could be tautological. They are
not. AUC depends only on the standardised gap
$(\mu_1-\mu_0)/\sqrt{v_1+v_0}$ and is invariant to rescaling both populations
together. $\varepsilon$ is not scale-invariant and grows with the variance
ratio. So a corruption that inflates $v$ drives AUC to $\tfrac12$ while
$\varepsilon$ keeps climbing. That is the masking regime, and it is why the two
can be traded against each other.

\paragraph{Corruptions.} Applied to the forget tokens $(x_f,y_f)$ only:
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{llll}
\toprule
name & edit & parameter & channel\\
\midrule
C1 & $y_f \to y_f + \epsilon$, \ $\epsilon\sim\mathcal N(0,\vs)$ & $\vs$ & variance\\
C2 & $x_f \to x_f + \epsilon$, \ $\epsilon\sim\mathcal N(0,\vs I)$ & $\vs$ & variance\\
C3 & both, independent draws & $\vs$ & variance\\
flip & $y_f \to (1-2t)\,y_f$ & $t$ & mean\\
bern & $y_f \to (1-2B)\,y_f$, \ $B\sim\mathrm{Bern}(\theta)$ & $\theta$ & mean + variance\\
whiten & $x_f \to$ covariance-matched to retain groups & mixing & --- \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The last two matter most. \texttt{flip} has a mean shift and no variance.
\texttt{bern} has the same mean, $\mathbb E[(1-2B)y]=(1-2\theta)y$, plus
variance $4\theta(1-\theta)y^2$. Write the predicted AUC as
$\Phi\!\big((\mu_1-\mu_0)/\sqrt{v_1+v_0}\big)$. A zero-mean corruption can only
inflate the denominator, so it can never push AUC through $\tfrac12$. A mean
shift moves the numerator directly.

\section{Method}

\needspacehack
\begin{algbox}
\algtitle{Algorithm 1 — measuring removal and preservation}
\small
\textbf{Input:} group spec, forget group $f$, corruption mode and grid\\[3pt]
\textbf{1.} Train two shadow ensembles of $S=512$ linear-attention models:
$\Mfull$ on all groups, $\Morac$ on the retain groups only.\\
\textbf{2.} Draw a probe of $P=64$ prompts and \emph{freeze} it.\\
\textbf{3.} For each corruption strength $t$ in the grid:\\
\hspace*{1.4em}(a) corrupt the forget tokens of the probe $\to$ $\tilde X$;\\
\hspace*{1.4em}(b) run \emph{both} ensembles on the same $\tilde X$;\\
\hspace*{1.4em}(c) $\mathrm{AUC}(t)$ from the two residual populations;\\
\hspace*{1.4em}(d) $\varepsilon(t)$ from the fitted residual laws;\\
\hspace*{1.4em}(e) \emph{shared-noise control}: repeat (a)--(c) broadcasting one
noise draw across all $S$ shadows. This holds variance inflation fixed, so the
gap between the two runs separates \emph{masking} from genuine removal.\\
\textbf{4.} Repeat for $3\times3$ (training seed, probe seed) combinations;
report medians.
\end{algbox}

Nothing retrains inside the loop. The corruption is a prompt-time edit, so a
whole sweep takes seconds against cached ensembles.

\paragraph{A closed form for the flip.} AUC is not differentiable through the
sampling, which is why a REINFORCE estimator is the usual choice. For this
policy it can be avoided. With $a_i = y_i\,(c_x\!\cdot\! x_i)$ over the forget
slice --- the signed contribution of context token $i$ to
\eqref{eq:readout} --- the change in the read-out has moments
\begin{equation}
\mathbb E[\Delta\hat y] = \frac{-2\theta}{N+1}\sum_i a_i,
\qquad
\mathrm{Var}[\Delta\hat y] = \frac{4\theta(1-\theta)}{(N+1)^2}\sum_i a_i^2 ,
\label{eq:moments}
\end{equation}
exactly, because $(1-2B)^2=1$ leaves the label--label block of the context
vector unchanged. Substituting \eqref{eq:moments} into the Gaussian AUC makes
$\mathrm{AUC}(\theta)$ a differentiable function of $\theta$. Monte Carlo at
$4\times10^5$ trials per point confirms both moments to 3--4 significant
figures.

\begin{algbox}
\algtitle{Algorithm 2 — learning the corruption (Stage 2)}
\small
The stated objective is $\min_\theta \mathrm{AUC}(\theta)+\lambda\|\theta\|$.
Minimising AUC overshoots. Past $\tfrac12$ the attack inverts, and $0.30$ is as
good an adversary as $0.70$. We minimise distance from chance instead:
\begin{equation*}
\min_\theta\ \big(\mathrm{AUC}(\theta)-\tfrac12\big)^2 + \lambda\|\theta\| .
\end{equation*}
\textbf{1.} Initialise $\theta$. \ \textbf{2.} Compute $\mathrm{AUC}(\theta)$
from \eqref{eq:moments}. \ \textbf{3.} Backpropagate, take an Adam step.
\ \textbf{4.} Repeat for 400 steps. \ \textbf{5.} Report AUC and $\varepsilon$
as measured by Algorithm 1, not the closed-form value.
\end{algbox}

\small
\textbf{Reading the numbers.}
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}\setlength{\topsep}{2pt}
\item $\mathrm{AUC}=\tfrac12$ is the \emph{goal} --- the adversary guessing.
The baseline sits \emph{above} $\tfrac12$ (a working attack) and unlearning
means driving it \emph{down} to $\tfrac12$. Overshooting below $\tfrac12$ is
not success either: the attack has merely inverted, and
$|\mathrm{AUC}-\tfrac12|$ remains the signal.
\item $\varepsilon$ small is good. It is a KL against the retrain oracle, so
$\varepsilon\!\to\!0$ means ``indistinguishable from having retrained properly''.
\item \textbf{Cost ratio} $=\varepsilon$ at the closest approach to chance,
divided by $\varepsilon$ at its own minimum. It measures how far apart those
two operating points are. A ratio near 1 would mean a clean operating point
exists.
\item Every value is a median over nine (training seed, probe seed)
combinations; bands are min--max across those nine.
\end{itemize}
\end{mdframed}

\section{Experiment 2 — replication on real data}

\subsection*{How MNIST enters the experiment}

MNIST supplies the input distributions, not the task. This is not digit
classification. The construction is stated in full below because it is easy to
assume otherwise.

\paragraph{Three groups.} Take digits $1$, $3$ and $8$; PCA the \emph{pooled}
set down to $D=4$ so all three share one feature space; split back by digit;
centre each class and normalise it so $\operatorname{tr}(\mathrm{Cov})=1$. The
result is three banks of 4-dimensional vectors, roughly $6.7$k, $6.1$k and
$5.9$k rows, one per image. A group is a cloud of thousands of points. Because
the traces are equal, the clouds differ only in covariance shape, summarised by
the participation ratio
$\mathrm{PR}=(\operatorname{tr}\Lambda)^2/\operatorname{tr}(\Lambda^2)$:
$2.44$, $3.29$, $2.93$. Retain $=\{d_1,d_3\}$, forget $=d_8$. The forget group
is the middle one, not an extreme. Unlike the synthetic setup, the geometry
here is whatever the data provided.

\paragraph{The labels come from a random plane, not from the digit.} For each
prompt we draw a fresh $\beta\sim\mathcal N(0,I_4)$ and set
$y=\mathrm{sign}(\beta^{\!\top}x)$. The label alphabet is fixed at $\pm1$, but
the labelling function changes every prompt, so the same image gets different
labels in different prompts. A fixed image-to-digit rule could be memorised in
the weights, leaving nothing to learn in context. A fresh $\beta$ forces the
model to recover the plane from the examples in front of it.

\paragraph{$\beta$ is shared across the three groups.} This constraint is
essential. If each digit had its own labelling rule, corrupting the forget
group would delete the answer rather than the membership evidence, and a fall
in AUC would measure nothing. With $\beta$ shared, all three groups obey one
rule and differ only in input geometry, which is the quantity the attack is
asked to detect. Sharing also keeps the retrain oracle competent on
forget-group queries. It can label a $d_8$ point correctly, having never seen
that class's geometry in training. If the digit were the label, the oracle
could not answer at all, and membership inference would reduce to detecting a
missing capability.

\paragraph{Probe layout.} Each of the $P=64$ prompts is
\begin{center}\small
$\underbrace{[\,10\ \text{from } d_1\,][\,10\ \text{from } d_3\,][\,11\ \text{from } d_8\,]}_{N=31\ \text{context tokens}}
\ \longrightarrow\ \underbrace{1\ \text{more } d_8\ \text{point}}_{\text{query}}$
\end{center}
Forget tokens are placed last so they form a contiguous slice. The query is
drawn from the forget group, so the model is asked about the distribution we
are trying to make it forget. That is where the membership signal appears.
Every corruption arm edits only those 11 $d_8$ context tokens, never $d_1$,
never $d_3$, never the query. Everything else is unchanged from Experiment 1:
the two $512$-model ensembles, the attack, the sweep, and the shared-noise
control.

\paragraph{Result 7.} This does not transfer to the synthetic task. There the

\paragraph{Result 8, and a closed form for $\theta^*$.} The optimum flips
$68$--$82\%$ of forget labels. This is not a small perturbation. It is the
mean-compensating solution. Setting the corrupted mean gap to zero,
$\delta_0 + \mathbb E[\Delta\hat y] = 0$ with $\mathbb E[\Delta\hat y]$ from

\begin{equation}
\theta^* \;=\; \frac{(N+1)\,\delta_0}{2\sum_i a_i},
\label{eq:thetastar}
\end{equation}

variance term. The extra spread $4\theta(1-\theta)\sum a_i^2$ sits in the
denominator of the AUC and cannot move the point where the numerator
vanishes.

deterministic \texttt{flip} at strength $t$ has the same mean shift and no
variance, so its AUC must cross $\tfrac12$ at exactly $t=\theta^*$. Reading

Table~\ref{tab:thetastar} explains Result 7. Cancelling the baseline gap on
synthetic ATTN-S requires $t=1.011$. But $\theta$ is a probability, so
$\theta\le1$. The required mean cancellation is outside the range a label flip
can deliver, so \texttt{bern} cannot reach chance there at all. It never does,
bottoming out at $0.5021$. The optimiser returning $\theta^*=0.895\pm0.091$ is
not converging badly. It is saturating against a bound. Synthetic ATTN-M sits
just inside the feasible region, and is correspondingly noisy.

This also explains the estimator disagreement. Pushing $\theta^*$ toward 1
drives the flip's mean shift, a sum of $n_f=11$ Bernoulli terms, into its
degenerate corner, where the variance vanishes and the standardised third
moment diverges. At the fitted optima the skew of that sum is $-0.37$ and
$-0.49$ on MNIST, against $-0.33$ and $-0.78$ on synthetic. On the worst
synthetic seed ($\theta=0.964$) it reaches $-1.50$, with excess kurtosis
$+2.07$. So the Gaussian AUC behind \eqref{eq:moments} is weakest exactly where
the synthetic optimum is forced to sit. These are equal-weight values. Unequal
$a_i$ make the skew larger, and the synthetic task's continuous labels give a
much wider spread of $a_i$ than MNIST's $\pm1$.

\section{Limitations}

\paragraph{Scope.} This is group-level unlearning. The adversary detects
whether a distribution was in training, not whether a particular example was.
Two datasets, one architecture family, and only the scalar policy. The
conditional policy $p_\theta(\cdot\mid x_f,y_f)$ is implemented but not
reported.

\paragraph{Where the method stops working.} Result 9 gives a hard boundary: if
cancelling the baseline gap needs a flip fraction above one, no label flip can
do it. Synthetic ATTN-S is on the wrong side of that boundary. The obvious
next step is to polish the closed-form optimum against measured AUC, which
would help where the Gaussian approximation is weak but cannot help where the
optimum is infeasible.

\paragraph{What the moment argument does not predict.} It predicts where the
optimum sits, not what it costs. Result 5 shows the flip's cost advantage
varies by a factor of three between datasets and does not track the baseline
gap.

\paragraph{Code and reproduction.} All code, configs and figures are at
\url{https://github.com/manyagupta13/icl-unlearning} (branch
\texttt{followup-experiments}). Every number in this report comes from
\texttt{artifacts/results\_regression.csv}, \texttt{artifacts/results\_mnist.csv}
and the Stage 2 JSONs, and each is checked against those files
programmatically. The full pipeline is four commands:

\section{Derivation of the flip moments and of $\theta^*$}
\label{app:deriv}

Equation~\eqref{eq:moments} is stated in Section 2 without proof. The
derivation is four steps.

\paragraph{Step 1 --- the read-out is linear in the context labels.} For linear
attention performing in-context regression, the prediction at the query is a
weighted sum of the context labels,
\[
\hat y_q \;=\; \frac{1}{N+1}\sum_i y_i\,(c_x\!\cdot\! x_i),
\qquad c_x = M^{\!\top} x_q .
\]
Write $a_i = y_i\,(c_x\!\cdot\!x_i)$ for the signed contribution of context
token $i$, so $\hat y_q = \frac{1}{N+1}\sum_i a_i$. Linearity in $y$ is the
only property used below.

\paragraph{Step 2 --- what the flip does.} The policy replaces $y_i$ by
$(1-2B_i)y_i$ for $i$ in the forget slice, with $B_i\sim\mathrm{Bern}(\theta)$
drawn independently. Token $i$'s contribution becomes $(1-2B_i)a_i$, so the
change in the prediction is
\[
\Delta\hat y \;=\; \frac{1}{N+1}\sum_{i\in f}\big[(1-2B_i)-1\big]a_i
\;=\; \frac{-2}{N+1}\sum_{i\in f} B_i\,a_i .
\]
A weighted sum of independent Bernoulli variables.

\paragraph{Step 3 --- its moments.} With $\mathbb E[B_i]=\theta$ and
$\mathrm{Var}(B_i)=\theta(1-\theta)$, independence gives
\eqref{eq:moments} immediately:
\[
\mathbb E[\Delta\hat y] = \frac{-2\theta}{N+1}\sum_i a_i,
\qquad
\mathrm{Var}[\Delta\hat y] = \frac{4\theta(1-\theta)}{(N+1)^2}\sum_i a_i^2 .
\]

\paragraph{Step 4 --- why this is exact, not first-order.} The context vector
also carries a label--label block, terms in $\sum_i y_i^2$. Under the flip
those become $\sum_i (1-2B_i)^2 y_i^2 = \sum_i y_i^2$, because $(1-2B)^2 = 1$
for $B\in\{0,1\}$. The block is unchanged, so no second-order correction
survives and the moments hold exactly for every $\theta$. Compare additive
noise, $y\to y+\epsilon$, which sends
$\sum y^2 \to \sum y^2 + 2\sum y\epsilon + \sum\epsilon^2$ and leaves terms
behind.

\paragraph{Consequence 1 --- a differentiable AUC.} Modelling the two residual
populations as Gaussian, $\mathrm{AUC} = \Phi\big((\mu_1-\mu_0)/\sqrt{v_1+v_0}\big)$,
and \eqref{eq:moments} supplies $\mu_1(\theta)$ and $v_1(\theta)$ in closed
form. So $\mathrm{AUC}(\theta)$ is an explicit smooth function and can be
differentiated directly, with no sampling and no gradient variance. That is
Algorithm 2.

\paragraph{Consequence 2 --- the optimum.} Setting the corrupted mean gap to
zero, $\delta_0 + \mathbb E[\Delta\hat y] = 0$, and solving gives
\eqref{eq:thetastar}, $\theta^* = (N+1)\delta_0 / (2\sum_i a_i)$. The variance
term does not appear. It sits in the denominator of the AUC and cannot move the
point where the numerator vanishes. That is why the deterministic \texttt{flip},
which has the same mean shift and no variance, must cross $\tfrac12$ at the
same parameter value. Table~\ref{tab:thetastar} tests that prediction.

\paragraph{Verification.} \texttt{tests/verify\_bern.py} draws $4\times10^5$
Monte Carlo realisations per grid point and recovers both moments to 3--4
significant figures, and confirms $\sum\tilde y^2 - \sum y^2 = 0$ exactly.

\subsection*{The two estimators, side by side}

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{p{0.44\textwidth}p{0.44\textwidth}}
\toprule
\textbf{Closed form (Algorithm 2)} & \textbf{REINFORCE (the brief)}\\
\midrule
Uses the formula for $\mathrm{AUC}(\theta)$ above and differentiates it.
& Never uses the formula. Samples masks and infers the slope from
measurements.\\
\addlinespace

& Per step: draw 32 masks, apply each, run both 512-model ensembles, weight by

\addlinespace
